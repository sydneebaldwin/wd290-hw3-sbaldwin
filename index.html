With names like Monet, Degas, and Van Gogh, journeying through the first works of impressionism is vital to understanding the history of art as a whole.

The Impressionist Revolution: Monet to Matisse takes you though nearly fifty artworks essential to the development of contemporary art — especially in regards to painting. The revolutionary techniques created by impressionist artists include plein-air painting, use of visible brush strokes rather than soft blending, and use of vibrant, saturated colors, even when conveying everyday life.

The impressionist movement rose in tandem with the popularity of photography, causing artists to shift from capturing staunch realism to reveling in these painterly techniques. Despite outcry from art critics and traditional painters of the time, impressionism gained traction and admiration across the art world, later inspiring post-impressionist, fauvist, and cubist art movements.

featured artists

claude monet

Early founder of impressionism, whose work had a direct influence on the rise of modernism.

edgar degas

Brought variety to impressionism's many nature-inspired works via his paintings of dancers.

berthe morisot

Best known for her use of multiple mediums in a single work, and her limited color palettes.

camille pissaro

Known for his paintings of mundane daily life, and his assssembly of a collective of impressionist artists.

vincent van gogh

Oil painter known best for his bold brushwork and expressive self portraits.

paul gauguin

Post-impressionist best known for his experimental use of color.

piet mondrian

Known for conveying utopian themes through his art, and utilization of many abstract elements.

henri matisse

Painter known for vibrant use of color and influence on Fauvism.
